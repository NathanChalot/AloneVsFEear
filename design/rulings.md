# Rulings

Our interpretations where the book and the [creator answers](creator-answers.md) are silent or ambiguous. Each ruling says what we decided and why.

- **Hunting works in any box.** "You may encounter that creature in your next box" applies to visited and unexplored boxes alike. A creature hunting you does not care whether you have been there before.
- **An Injury or Mental Scar negates the whole loss.** The main rules say it prevents "the loss of 1 or 2 points" (`1_1_main_rules.md`, Sanity and Life), the combat chapter says it negates "all Damage from a single attack" (`3_combat.md`, Mental Scars and Injuries). We follow the combat chapter: it is less punitive and leaves room for spectacular moments (losing an arm to survive a massive blow). The Zombie Tiger ("if you already have 3 Injuries, lose 3 Life instead") supports this reading. This ruling should stay switchable: the strict reading (absorb at most 2 points) could be a harder game mode.
- **Expert costs the Skill's XP cost again.** The book never states the upgrade cost. Learning the Basic Skill then paying the same cost again for Expert follows "skills may be added at any moment in the game, after paying their XP cost" (`1_2_skills.md`) and "some Arcane Skills may be learned twice (they have an Expert level)" (`1_3_arcane_skills.md`). Matches a community answer on BoardGameGeek.

## Switchable rulings

Some rulings are a matter of taste and could become campaign options chosen at campaign start (e.g. a strict mode and a cinematic mode). Not designed yet: for now, mark switchable rulings as such.
- **Hands are extra slots.** The 2 hand slots come on top of the carry limit, as the encumbrance summary reads ("1 object in each hand… you may also carry 10 objects"). Switchable: a horrific mode could count them.
- **Strong raises items only, not the Food limit.** The Food rules mention only the backpack. A Strong character can still carry more Food through the extra item slots (1 Food = 1 item).
- **Discarded items stay in the box, at a risk.** When coming back to a box where items were left, roll d6: on 1-3 they are gone. Mirrors the stash theft rule. Switchable: a horrific mode could make them gone at once.
- **Ammo without a matching weapon is kept.** Ammo is not an item and costs no slot. Leaving it behind follows the discarded items rule.
- **Max 3 Safe Houses.** The Street table says "if it is the third", the other tables "if there are already 3". We follow the majority.

## Combat

From the combat design session (2026-10-30). Rules the book states are in [combat](combat.md), not here.

- **Natural 1 and natural 6 apply to every roll vs L, Attack included.** The sentence sits under Saves: "Rolling a 1 is always a failure and rolling a 6 is always a success, no matter what modifiers are involved" (`1_1_main_rules.md`). We checked every rule that mentions rolling a 1: none says an Attack on a 1 still hits (kitchen knife, replica sword, flail, raygun only add a side effect), and creatures that hit harder on a Defense 1 (Master Scarecrow, Giant Worm) assume the 1 fails. On Attack, failure means 0 hits and success means at least 1 hit. A specific text that says otherwise wins. Search is not a roll vs L and has its own wording ("A roll of 1 remains a 1").
- **The player shares Damage among members with more than 1 Life.** The book covers 1-Life members ("each Damage point kills one of them", the shotgun example) and lets the grenade's Damage be "divided in any desired way" (`3_combat.md`, `8_weapon_summary_table.md`). It does not say how Damage spreads over 2-Life Howlers. We let the player share it, extra Damage going on to the next member.
- **Damage taken goes through armor, then Luck, then Injury, then Life.** Each step is in the book, the order is not. Armor rolls "every time you take Damage" (`2_2_useful_stuff_table.md`), so it comes first. Armor takes the Damage up to its remaining Life and the rest goes on, also not written. The Injury comes last because it is the most costly and only matters if Damage is left.
- **Left-behind creatures keep their wounds.** The Slasher and the Zombie Abominion come back "fully healed" as an explicit exception (`5_24_slashers_table.md`, `5_31_unique_zombies_table.md`), which suggests the default is that they do not. Switchable.
- **A failed Stealth roll buffs only the group it was rolled against.** Each group gets its own Stealth roll ([creator answers](creator-answers.md)), and the +1 L is "its L", "the enemy" (`3_combat.md`).
- **Waves are separate combats.** Each wave has its own surprise roll, its own "for the whole combat" effects and its own XP.
- **Escape with no visited neighbour goes to any neighbour.** The book sends you "back to any neighboring box that you have already visited" (`3_combat.md`, Escape rolls), which does not exist in the first box. There you choose any neighbour and explore it as a new box.
- **Escaping several groups is all or nothing.** Roll Escape against each group in the fight. If any roll fails you stay in the box, and next round every group is back in the fight, the ones you got away from included. Each group you failed against inflicts the Damage of one of its creatures, and that is its turn. Only the groups you failed against act that round; the ones you got away from do not attack. An encounter with several creature profiles (Kalina and her toy dinosaur) is several groups. Switchable: the book only describes escaping one group.
- **A failed Escape costs one creature's Damage per group**, not one per member: "you automatically take the monster's Damage… it makes no difference whether you are running away from a single opponent or a group of monsters" (`3_combat.md`, Escape rolls).
- **One action is your whole turn.** Attack, Escape, use an item, cast, change or draw a weapon, stand up: one of them per turn. What the book calls free (firing a curse, a knot reroll, Luck points) costs nothing. The book uses "1 action" and "1 turn" for the same costs, and the grenade lets you "use it on the following turn without having to spend a turn to draw it". The rifle's double attack is an exception written on the weapon.
- **Escaping during waves: you escape every group or none.** You Stealth each group, and keep rolling after a failure. You fight the groups you failed against in waves. To Escape, you must succeed against every group in the box, fought yet or not, snuck past or not. Only the groups you failed the Escape against deal Damage (one creature's each). If any roll fails, from the next round every group fights you in waves: the failed-Stealth groups first, the snuck-past groups after. See [combat](combat.md).
