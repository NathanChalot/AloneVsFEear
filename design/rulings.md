# Rulings

Our decisions where the book and the [creator answers](creator-answers.md) do not settle a rule. Rules the book states clearly live in the topic pages, with their source. Each ruling says what we decided and why, and carries tags:

- `interpretation` — the book says something, but its meaning or scope is unclear. A creator answer or an errata could overrule it.
- `gap` — the book says nothing. The rule is ours until the book or the creator says otherwise.
- `switchable` — a matter of taste that could become a campaign option chosen at campaign start (e.g. a strict or horrific mode). Campaign options are not designed yet.

## Map

See [town map](town-map.md).

- **Distance counts moves, a diagonal step counting as 1.** `interpretation` The book counts "the number of boxes on the town map that you should cross to reach that location" (Safe House results) and lets you "move diagonally if you wish" (`5_how_the_game_works.md`). The boxes you should cross are the fewest you walk through, so a diagonal neighbour is 1 box away. It also keeps #16's "1 or 2 boxes" in line with moves. Counting only orthogonal steps is the other reading; the two can pick a different closest Safe House.
- **The town is one grid; sheets attach on seams.** `interpretation` Sheets are added "next to the previous ones" (`1_1_main_rules.md`), so boxes across a seam are neighbours and distances cross sheets. The player picks the side.
- **A sheet's open edges are walls until it is full.** `interpretation` A new sheet is attached when "you visit all the boxes on a sheet" (`5_how_the_game_works.md`), or after a mission ([creator answers](creator-answers.md)).
- **A new sheet attaches against any full sheet.** `interpretation` The book says to attach a sheet once you have visited all the boxes on a sheet, not which one. The new sheet shares a side with at least one full sheet, the latest or an earlier one.
- **The Food clock is the campaign's box number.** `interpretation` One Food is eaten whenever the box number is a multiple of 5. Boxes do not reset between missions ([creator answers](creator-answers.md)), and boxes are also time, so a new character after a death shares the schedule of the one before (their first meal may come sooner than 5 boxes after they start).
- **Hunting works in any box.** `interpretation` "You may encounter that creature in your next box" applies to visited and unexplored boxes alike. A creature hunting you does not care whether you have been there before.

## Character

- **An Injury or Mental Scar negates the whole loss.** `interpretation` `switchable` The main rules say it prevents "the loss of 1 or 2 points" (`1_1_main_rules.md`, Sanity and Life), the combat chapter says it negates "all Damage from a single attack" (`3_combat.md`, Mental Scars and Injuries). We follow the combat chapter: it is less punitive and leaves room for spectacular moments (losing an arm to survive a massive blow). The Zombie Tiger ("if you already have 3 Injuries, lose 3 Life instead") supports this reading. Switchable: the strict reading (absorb at most 2 points) could be a harder game mode.
- **At 0 Life and 0 Sanity at once, the character is dead.** `gap` The book ends the game at 0 Life (dead) and at 0 Sanity (insane) but never says which wins when both happen together. Either way the game is lost; only the ending differs. Death is checked first.
- **Expert costs the Skill's XP cost again.** `gap` The book never states the upgrade cost. Learning the Basic Skill then paying the same cost again for Expert follows "skills may be added at any moment in the game, after paying their XP cost" (`1_2_skills.md`) and "some Arcane Skills may be learned twice (they have an Expert level)" (`1_3_arcane_skills.md`). Matches a community answer on BoardGameGeek.

## Inventory

- **Hands are extra slots.** `interpretation` `switchable` The 2 hand slots come on top of the carry limit, as the encumbrance summary reads ("1 object in each hand… you may also carry 10 objects"). Switchable: a horrific mode could count them.
- **Strong raises items only, not the Food limit.** `gap` The Food rules mention only the backpack. A Strong character can still carry more Food through the extra item slots (1 Food = 1 item).
- **Discarded items stay in the box, at a risk.** `gap` `switchable` When coming back to a box where items were left, roll d6: on 1-3 they are gone. Mirrors the stash theft rule. They disappear when the character dies, like the stash: what the character owns follows the character. Switchable: a horrific mode could make them gone at once.
- **Ammo without a matching weapon is kept.** `gap` Ammo is not an item and costs no slot. Leaving it behind follows the discarded items rule.

## Safe Houses

- **Max 3 Safe Houses.** `interpretation` The Street table says "if it is the third", the other tables "if there are already 3". We follow the majority.
- **Home is a Safe House.** `gap` `switchable` The book never says what Home is. The visited-box rule ("Roll a 1 in 6 chance of a Random Encounter", `5_how_the_game_works.md`) makes no exception for it, and Bring Home the Bacon names "your starting box (or in any Safe House)" side by side. We read Home as the place the character has barricaded: they start the game with somewhere to heal, with no random encounter there. Decided in the E5a session; the mechanics come with Safe Houses (E15), together with how much Home heals and whether it counts toward the 3 Safe Houses. Switchable: a horrific mode makes Home a plain box, with the 1 in 6 Random Encounter.

## Combat

See [combat](combat.md).

- **Natural 1 and natural 6 apply to every roll vs L, Attack included.** `interpretation` The sentence sits under Saves: "Rolling a 1 is always a failure and rolling a 6 is always a success, no matter what modifiers are involved" (`1_1_main_rules.md`). We checked every rule that mentions rolling a 1: none says an Attack on a 1 still hits (kitchen knife, replica sword, flail, raygun only add a side effect), and creatures that hit harder on a Defense 1 (Master Scarecrow, Giant Worm) assume the 1 fails. On Attack, failure means 0 hits and success means at least 1 hit. A specific text that says otherwise wins. Search is not a roll vs L and has its own wording ("A roll of 1 remains a 1").
- **The player shares Damage among members with more than 1 Life.** `gap` The book covers 1-Life members ("each Damage point kills one of them", the shotgun example) and lets the grenade's Damage be "divided in any desired way" (`3_combat.md`, `8_weapon_summary_table.md`). It does not say how Damage spreads over 2-Life Howlers. The player shares it, extra Damage going on to the next member.
- **Damage taken goes through armor, then Luck, then Injury, then Life.** `gap` Each step is in the book, the order is not. Armor rolls "every time you take Damage" (`2_2_useful_stuff_table.md`), so it comes first. Armor takes the Damage up to its remaining Life and the rest goes on, also not written. The Injury comes last because it is the most costly and only matters if Damage is left.
- **One action is your whole turn.** `interpretation` Attack, Escape, use an item, cast, change or draw a weapon, stand up: one of them per turn. What the book calls free (firing a curse, a knot reroll, Luck points) costs nothing. The book uses "1 action" and "1 turn" for the same costs, and the grenade lets you "use it on the following turn without having to spend a turn to draw it". The rifle's double attack is an exception written on the weapon.
- **Left-behind creatures keep their wounds.** `interpretation` `switchable` The Slasher and the Zombie Abominion come back "fully healed" as an explicit exception (`5_24_slashers_table.md`, `5_31_unique_zombies_table.md`), which suggests the default is that they do not.
- **A failed Stealth roll buffs only the group it was rolled against.** `interpretation` Each group gets its own Stealth roll ([creator answers](creator-answers.md)), and the +1 L is "its L", "the enemy" (`3_combat.md`).
- **All the waves in a box are one combat, with XP per group.** `gap` The creator says groups are fought "in waves, one after the other with no rest between waves" but not whether that is one combat. Start and end of combat triggers happen once for the box (the grenade already says "roll only once… in this encounter"), and each group was its own encounter, so each defeated group gives its XP. Effects that last "the current combat" end with each wave (Dodge's -1 to your next Attack). Effects tied to a creature (Charisma -1 on the first Attack against it, Stealth +1 L) stay with that creature. Resetting is the kinder option; in the book it only affects Dodge.
- **A failed Escape costs one creature's Damage per group**, not one per member. `interpretation` "You automatically take the monster's Damage… it makes no difference whether you are running away from a single opponent or a group of monsters" (`3_combat.md`, Escape rolls).
- **Escaping several groups is all or nothing.** `gap` `switchable` Roll Escape against each group in the fight. If any roll fails you stay in the box, and next round every group is back in the fight, the ones you got away from included. Each group you failed against inflicts the Damage of one of its creatures, and that is its turn; the ones you got away from do not attack that round. An encounter with several creature profiles (Kalina and her toy dinosaur) is several groups. The book only describes escaping one group.
- **Escaping during waves: you escape every group or none.** `gap` You Stealth each group, and keep rolling after a failure. You fight the groups you failed against in waves. To Escape, you must succeed against every group in the box, fought yet or not, snuck past or not. Only the groups you failed the Escape against deal Damage (one creature's each). If any roll fails, from the next round every group fights you in waves: the failed-Stealth groups first, the snuck-past groups after.
- **Escape with no visited neighbour goes to any neighbour.** `gap` The book sends you "back to any neighboring box that you have already visited" (`3_combat.md`, Escape rolls), which does not exist in the first box. There you choose any neighbour and explore it as a new box.

## Encounters

See [encounters](encounters.md).

- **Ignoring an encounter blocks the Search.** `interpretation` You may Search "only after dealing with whatever encounter, danger, or situation you encounter in that area" (`5_4_town_exploration_table.md`); ignored creatures are still there, so they were not dealt with. To Search, fight them.
- **Moving along blocks the Search.** `interpretation` "You may explore it or move along" (House 5, Clinic 5, Farm 1): moving along means walking past without entering, as if the box was skipped. Like ignoring, it does not deal with what is there, so the box cannot be Searched. The place stays in the box, like ignored creatures: on a later visit you may explore it, then Search.
- **Limits are campaign-wide, and numbered events happen once, unless the text says otherwise.** `interpretation` "Once in the game" (Special Events) and "twice in a game" (Woods/Park 1 and 6) count over the whole campaign; the book says "mission" when it means one (Fluid needle, Children's Stuff, events #9 and #10). A numbered event with no stated limit is played once per campaign; when it is reached again, the fallback written where it is called applies, or nothing happens. The callers support this: House 6 and Street 6 give a fallback "if you have already played" #1 and #3. Events meant to repeat say so (#2, #33). Weak point: "between games" in the main rules means between missions.
- **SNOWY is a campaign keyword.** `interpretation` The book writes keywords on "your play sheet", but SNOWY records a change in the town (new content becomes reachable), not something the character carries or knows. Following the town/person test ([encounters](encounters.md)), it outlives the character. CLOWNFACE describes the character and dies with them.
- **A box counts as every place table rolled in it.** `gap` When a place result chains to another place table (Clinic 1 → Haunted House), the box is a haunted clinic: it counts as a Clinic and as a Haunted House for rules that read the place ("every time you visit a Clinic box", Medical Supplies). If a rule needs a single place, the first one wins: the place given by the Town Exploration table. A box whose content never reached a place table (Town Exploration 2) has no place.
- **The "Useful Items table" of Search 6 is the Useful Stuff table.** `interpretation` No table has that name. Search 6 offers "the Useful Items table or the Random Object table", the same pair the play aids give at creation ("the Useful Stuff table or the Random Objects table"). The text stays as printed; its reference points to the Useful Stuff table (`5_1_search_rolls_table.md`).

## Effects

See [effects](effects.md).

- **Modifiers stack unless the text says otherwise.** `gap` The book limits stacking case by case: Agile and Dodge "use one or the other" (`1_2_skills.md`), "multiple amulets do not have cumulative effect" (`5_35_random_magic_items_table.md`), a bonus "not cumulative if you already have Good Shot" (`6_unique_events.md`). Writing down the exceptions suggests the default is to stack. Two curses at -1 on all rolls give -2. An online search found no creator answer.
- **A box of time without moving advances the clock only.** `gap` `switchable` Reading an occult book "uses d3 boxes of time", a ritual "takes 2 boxes of time" (`1_3_arcane_skills.md`). Each box of time raises the box number: Food, "for the next N boxes" effects and mission deadlines count it. It is not entering a box: no encounter roll, no place rules, and deadlines counting new boxes (Hell Gate) ignore it. Switchable: a harsher mode adds a 1 in 6 random encounter for each box of time, as when entering a visited box.
- **Reactions that help you come before reactions that hurt you.** `gap` `switchable` When several reactions fire on the same moment and the book gives no order, the helpful ones resolve first; among reactions of the same sort, the player picks. Entering a Safe House with the Sewers infection ("lose 1 Life every time you cross a box until you reach a Safe House", `5_14_sewers_table.md`): the Safe House cures you before the infection costs Life, so you cannot die on the doorstep. After a combat, the first aid kit heals you before the grenade's 3 in 6 encounter arrives: you are still bandaging when the creatures come. Better for the story and less frustrating. The Damage pipeline keeps its own order. Switchable: a horrific mode could resolve harmful reactions first.
