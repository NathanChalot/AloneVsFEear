    - Escape, and how a combat ends.

  - What it shows:
    - The design needs to remember the actual die face as well as the total,
      because many rules read the die face (exploding sixes, weapons that break
      on a 1).
    - Damage you take passes through several steps in order.
    - The player makes choices in the middle of a round, like whom to attack.
    - Combat needs no new kind of effect. It does need effects that last one
      combat or one attack, effects that apply to creatures, and creatures that
      keep their own state (current Life, wounds).

      each Defense roll, taking Damage…) as input for the "effects and game

⏺ Right now it holds three different things, and the file's own description
  ("our interpretations where the book… is silent or ambiguous") only covers two
  of them:

  1. Interpretations. The book says something, but the meaning or scope is
     unclear, so we pick a reading. Examples: a natural 1 fails on Attack too
     (the sentence sits under Saves), and an Injury negates the whole loss (two
     passages disagree).
  2. Gap fills. The book says nothing, so we invent a rule. Examples: the damage
     order (armor, Luck, Injury, Life), escaping several groups, how Damage
     spreads over 2-Life creatures.
  3. Variants. We deliberately allow an alternative to a rule, as a possible
     campaign option. Examples: the strict Injury mode, items in hands counting
     toward the carry limit. Today these are just marked "switchable" inside the
     other two kinds. The escape destination was briefly one of these before
     you went back to the book.

  The difference matters for the engine and for you as a player:
  - An interpretation can turn out wrong: a creator answer or an errata could
    overrule it.
  - A gap fill is ours until the book or the creator says otherwise.
  - A variant is a game option the player chooses, and the engine has to support
    both versions.

  My recommendation: keep a single rulings.md, but tag each ruling with its kind
  (interpretation, gap, variant) and keep the source quote. Rules the book
  states clearly stay in the topic pages, as we did today for Defense on a 1.
  Variants could later get their own file when we design campaign options, but
  that isn't worth deciding yet.

  Does that match how you see it, or did you mean rulings to be something
  narrower, for example interpretations only?

⏺ You've got one thing slightly off: the +1 L doesn't depend on this choice. We
  agreed that a failed Stealth roll gives +1 L only to the group you failed
  against. Fail against two groups and both have +1 L, whether the box counts as
  one combat or several. Snuck-past groups that join later never get it.

  The choice changes other things. Surprise isn't one of them: a failed Stealth
  roll already means that group acts first. What does change:

  1. XP. The book gives "1 XP per violent encounter". Each left-behind group was
     its own encounter when you first met it, so defeating 3 groups could give
     3 XP (one per group) or 1 XP (one combat).
  2. Start and end of combat triggers. Two examples:
     - The Howlers' Fear Save "at the beginning of the combat" happens either
       when their wave starts or once, whenever the box fight starts. Either way
       it's once per Howler group.
     - End-of-combat rolls happen once, after the whole box is cleared, instead
       of after each wave. That covers the chainsaw (1 in 6, d6 zombies), the
       grenade (3 in 6) and the Howlers (2 in 6). The grenade text already says
       "roll only once… in this encounter", which fits one combat.
  3. Effects "for the first attack" or "the whole combat" on your side would
     last for the whole box, not reset with each wave. Liquor, the Charisma -1
     and the Stealth +2 are examples.

⏺ That sentence was vague, and partly wrong. Concretely, the book has two kinds
  of effects with a combat-length duration.

  1. Effects that last "the current combat", the only kind where waves matter.
  Example: Dodge says a natural 1 on Defense gives you "-1 to your next Attack
  roll in the current combat".
  - Say you roll that 1 on your last Defense against wave 1.
  - One combat for the box: the -1 carries over to your first Attack against
    wave 2.
  - Separate combats: it would vanish when wave 1 ends.

  2. Effects tied to one creature. These don't care about waves either way:
  - The Charisma -1 applies to your "first Attack roll against that creature".
  - The Stealth +1 L belongs to the group you failed against, and lasts until
    that group is dead or you leave.

  So "first attack effects last for the whole box" was wrong. In the book, those
  effects follow the creature, not the wave.

  Suggested rewording for combat.md and the ruling:

  ▎ Effects that last "the current combat" (Dodge's -1 to your next Attack) 
  ▎ carry over from one wave to the next. Effects tied to a creature (Charisma 
  ▎ -1 on the first Attack against it, Stealth +1 L) stay with that creature.

  Shall I use this wording?
