⏺ Where we are
    You can create a character (the creation steps now explain themselves) and
  walk the town in the terminal. Each box's entry is shown as text and you apply
  it by hand, until you die or quit. You can spend XP on Skills at any time.
  Short drills (--drill) each try one feature in a break, and typing # at any
  prompt writes feedback. The last session was a planning session: books and
  Arcane Skills move into Step 1 (E18), after V1.

  This session: E9 — Inventory
    Goal: the character really carries things. Two hands, a carry limit (10, +5
  with a backpack, +3/+5 with Strong, never more than 20), and a Food limit (10,
  or 15 with a backpack), with extra Food taking item slots. Light items don't
  count, and ammo belongs to a weapon. You can drop items (they may vanish) and
  leave them at a Safe House. Losing the backpack makes you drop everything not
  in your hands. Items can carry effects (the backpack raises the limit). Item
  counts show their unit on screen (Rifle, 11 bullets).
    Done when: picking up, holding, dropping and stashing all respect every
  limit, with tests, and an inventory drill exists.

  How it will go
    This is a clear-spec code session (design/inventory.md, plus the rulings
  already made: hand slots don't count toward the carry limit, Strong doesn't
  raise the Food limit, dropped items vanish on a d6 roll of 1-3, ammo without a
  weapon is kept).
  1. A short plan: how the inventory sits in the game state, what Game gives
     (pick up, hold, drop, stash), how the backpack and Strong plug into
     Parameter.CARRY_LIMIT, and the unit for counts in the YAML.
  2. I'll point out anything the design doesn't cover, with options for you to
     choose from, and won't decide it myself.
  3. Tests first, then the code, then the inventory drill (in DRILLS and
     tests/test_drills.py).

⏺ Here's the plan, then a few questions whose answers change what we build.

  Plan (proposed)

  1. State. The character gets hands (0–2 items), items (carried, as today) and
     worn (for the backpack and clothes). Ammo becomes its own record (Q1
     below). A box gets items for what was left there. That list is the stash
     when the box is a Safe House, and the "left behind, may vanish" pile
     otherwise.
  2. Plain data in the YAML (Q2): how many slots an item takes (0 for
     "Negligible weight", 2 for smelting equipment), its slots when worn, the
     unit of its count (bullets, jars, doses), a weapon's hands (1 or 2), and
     its ammo type. A bundle (d6 jars of cat food) takes its slots once,
     whatever the count.
  3. Limits through effects. Strong's carry limit (+3/+5) goes into its YAML; it
     isn't wired yet, only its Strength roll is. The backpack gets +5 carry
     limit and +5 to a new FOOD_LIMIT parameter. The engine now reads effects
     from items held, too. The carry limit gets a ceiling of 20.
  4. Game API (low level, refuses what breaks a limit): room(), slots_used(),
     add_item/remove_item checked, hold/stow, wear/take_off,
     leave_item/pick_up_item, lose_backpack. Food over its limit takes slots.
  5. A pick-up flow (engine/inventory.py, the design's flowchart). It's used by
     creation, book mode and the box's left items. When there's no room, the
     engine asks: drop something (it stays in the box), or leave the new item.
  6. The terminal: counts shown with their unit (Rifle, 11 bullets), the sheet
     split into hands / carried (7 of 10) / worn / ammo. Then the inventory
     drill.

  Tests come first, at each step.

  Decide when implementing (my defaults, which you can change):
  - The spear-like weapons ("one or two handed") take 1 hand in E9. The grip is
    chosen in combat (E12).
  - The axe takes 1 hand with Strong (Expert).
  - The engine doesn't model eating Food items (cat food, energy drinks) yet:
    book mode applies it by hand (drop the item, gain Food).
  - Item effects with a lifetime (scarab, flowers) stay as text until their
    tasks. Only the backpack's effects are wired.
