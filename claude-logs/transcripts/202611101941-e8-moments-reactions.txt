⏺ Status: the online search found nothing on the rule, and the one designer Q&A
  thread on BoardGameGeek blocks automated reading. Here are the vocabulary
  refresher and the three points.

  Vocabulary

  - Moment: a point in play that the engine names and announces, so that rules
    can hook into it. Examples:
    - "A box of time passes": the box number goes up. The Food upkeep counts
      this, and so do "for the next 3 boxes" effects.
    - "You enter a box": you arrive somewhere. Safe House healing and a
      Sewers-style "lose 1 Life every time you cross a box" hook here.
  - Reaction: an effect that acts by itself when its moment comes, without the
    player choosing it. Examples:
    - The Food upkeep: each time a box of time passes, if a meal is due, you eat
      1 Food, or lose 1 Life if you have none.
    - Armor (later, E13): when you would take Damage, the armor rolls to absorb
      some.
  - Lifetimes count moments too. Painkillers giving "−1 on all rolls for the
    next 3 boxes" end after 3 "a box of time passes" moments.

  1. Where moments are announced: the gameplay impact

  Both options are engine code, and neither touches the screen directly. The
  only difference is whether the code running a reaction can ask the player
  something.

  - Today, almost no visible difference. You move to box 25 and you eat 1 Food.
    With the loop-level option, the meal becomes its own line after the arrival
    ("Box 25." then "You eat 1 Food, 3 left."). With the other option, it stays
    part of the arrival message, as now.
  - Later, it matters. Some reactions will need a choice from the player:
    - spending Luck to reduce Damage;
    - taking an Injury to avoid a loss (E13);
    - a reaction script with a choice step (E10).

    With reactions inside Game, those would mean reworking this code then. With
    the loop-level option, it's already ready for them. That's why I recommend
    the loop level.

  2. The Food clock

  Your reading matches "since the last meal". The next meal is always the last
  meal plus the current interval, recalculated whenever the interval changes. So
  a change affects the interval already running, not only the next one:

  - Resistant to Hunger: ate at 20, learn the Skill at 22 → the next meal moves
    from 25 to 26.
  - Eating Disorder: ate at 20, gain the Scar at 21 → the next meal moves from
    25 to 23.

  One case is left: the new interval is already used up. For example, you ate at
  20 and gain Eating Disorder at 24, so the meal was due at 23. Do you eat at
  the next box (25), or right away at 24? I'd say the next box, since meals
  happen when time passes. A new character keeps the campaign's schedule, as the
  current ruling already says.

  3. Resistant to Hunger with Eating Disorder

  I found no answer online. Our design/creator-answers.md only covers boxes not
  resetting between missions. The BoardGameGeek thread where the designer's
  answers are gathered refuses automated access. If you'd like, you could check
  it in a browser: Some rules answers and clarifications from the designer.

  Without an answer, the choice comes down to what it does in play:

  - Eating Disorder counts as −2, and Resistant to Hunger still adds. A scarred
    character with the Skill eats every 4 boxes, or every 5 at Expert, as an
    ordinary person would. Buying the Skill helps cope with the Scar. You could
    call that recovery through discipline.
  - Eating Disorder fixes the interval at 3. While scarred, the Skill does
    nothing, and XP spent on it after gaining the Scar is wasted. It's harsher,
    and arguably closer to "you require 1 Food every 3 boxes".

  Either way it goes into design/rulings.md and stays switchable. Shall we take
  point 1 first?

  2. The edge case: the meal is already overdue when the interval drops

  The setup: you ate at box 20, and in box 24 a Sanity loss gives you Eating
  Disorder. The interval drops to 3, so the meal was "due" at 23, which is
  already past. Two readings:

  In play
  (a) Eat when the next box of time passes: You finish what's happening in box
    24, and the meal comes on entering 25 (as it would have anyway).
  (b) Eat right away: The craving hits at once, mid-encounter: eat 1 Food now,
  or
    lose 1 Life right after the Sanity loss.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Feel
  (a) Eat when the next box of time passes: Steady: meals always happen as you
    move.
  (b) Eat right away: Dramatic and harsher: a Scar can chain into a Life loss in

    the same moment.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Engine
  (a) Eat when the next box of time passes: Nothing extra: the upkeep only runs
    when time passes.
  (b) Eat right away: The upkeep must also react to "the interval changed",
    meaning a check whenever effects change (a condition, not a moment). That's
    more machinery, which the design keeps for "until a condition" lifetimes.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Owed meals
  (a) Eat when the next box of time passes: Only ever one: from 5 down to 3, at
    most 4 boxes have passed.
  (b) Eat right away: Same.

  My recommendation is (a): it's simpler, it fits "every N boxes you cross", and
  it avoids a surprise Life loss stacked on a Sanity loss.

  Something else this shows about the screen: with a shifting interval, the
  player can't work out the next meal by eye any more. Showing "next meal: box
  26" in the character status would make the clock readable. It's cheap, and it
  costs nothing to decide now or later.

  Which reading do you want, (a) or (b)?

  6. The "add a lasting effect" manual action

  The situation. You're in box 10. Book mode shows an entry the engine can't

  2. The engine stores the note but never reads it.
  3. In box 11 you make a Search. Because you have a note, the engine asks

  4. In box 12 you make a manual Save roll and type -1 again.
  5. In box 14 you must remember that the painkillers wore off, and remove the
     note yourself.

  You do the bookkeeping: remembering the −1 on every roll, and remembering when
  it ends.

  With the new manual action (option a):
  1. In box 10 you pick "add a lasting effect". The engine asks three things:
     - What does it change? All rolls, or one roll type (Attack, Save, Search…).
     - By how much? −1.
     - For how long? For the next N boxes (you type 3), or until you remove it.
  2. In boxes 11 to 13, every roll collects the −1 by itself, named like the
     Skills are: −1 (lasting effect). Nothing to type.
  3. On entering box 14, the engine tells you "the −1 on all rolls has ended"
     and drops it.

  The engine does the bookkeeping. This is the same machinery E8 builds anyway
  for content ("for the next N boxes" effects). The manual action only lets you
  create one by hand from a text the engine can't read yet.
