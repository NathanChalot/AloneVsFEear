⏺ Let's take them in order.

  1. Does ignoring an encounter block the Search?

  What the book says:
  - "You may Search an area ONLY after dealing with whatever encounter, danger, 
    or situation you encounter in that area. If you flee from an encounter, you 
    may NOT Search that area." (5_4_town_exploration_table.md)
  - "Whenever you enter a box and you play an encounter or there is nothing in 
    the box, you may perform a Search roll." (5_how_the_game_works.md)

  A concrete case: House 3. "The house is attacked by 2d6 L2 Zombies. You may 
  ignore them or fight them." You choose to ignore them. You didn't flee,
  because there was no fight and no Escape roll. But you didn't really "deal
  with" the zombies either: they stay in the box. Can you Search the house?

  The book bans the Search after fleeing and is silent on ignoring. There are
  two readings:
  - Ignoring is a form of fleeing. The creatures are still there, so you can't
    search under their noses.
  - Ignoring is a way of dealing with it. You chose not to engage, and the ban
    applies only to an actual escape.

  "Move along" is a related case. The Dilapidated House says "You may explore it
  or move along." If you move along, nothing was met at all. The text suggests
  you walk past without entering. Is the box then like "nothing in the box"
  (Search allowed), or did you skip it entirely?

  Why I raised it: our own docs already contradict each other. The glossary
  lists "ignored" among the outcomes that resolve an encounter, which allows the
  Search. The arrival flowchart in game-flow.md puts "fled or ignored" in the
  same branch, which doesn't allow it.

  Does it matter for the design? Only a little. The model needs to record how an
  encounter ended (resolved, ignored, fled) rather than just "resolved or not".
  Which of those outcomes allows the Search is a one-line rule we can set
  later. What should be fixed now is the contradiction in our docs: they should
  say "ignored is its own outcome, Search rule to decide" instead of quietly
  taking a side.

  How do you see this one? Shall we fix the docs that way and move to question
  2?
