  What drawing the flows taught me

  Writing these out showed four structural facts. I think they matter more than
  any of the D-decisions:

  1. Two mechanisms carry the whole game: roll vs L and roll on a table. Table
     results chain into other tables, events and choices.
  2. Modifiers come from everywhere: Skills, items, Injuries, Scars, the mission
     and temporary effects. A roll has to collect all of them.
  3. Rules react to moments. Missions are mostly made of these reactions: "every
     time you visit a Clinic", "after 12+d6 boxes", "the last 5 locations",
     "every 5 boxes". So the design needs a concept of game moments that rules
     can hook into. That's where I'd expect the hard architecture problems, not
     in file formats.
  4. The player chooses all the time. The engine asks and the interface answers.
     This also makes the CLI vs GUI vs genAI question much easier later.

  2. The Injury / Scar ambiguity

  The mechanic: when you're about to lose Life, you may instead roll on the
  Injury table and take a permanent Injury. Sanity works the same way with the
  Insanity table and Mental Scars. The question is how much loss one Injury or 
  Scar cancels.

  - Main rules (gameRules/1_1_main_rules.md:122 for Sanity, :130 for Life): you
    "prevent the loss of 1 or 2 points".
  - Combat chapter (gameRules/3_combat.md:86-88, "Mental Scars and Injuries"):
    for Sanity, "prevent that Sanity loss" (the whole loss). For Life, "negating
    all Damage from a single attack".

  Concrete case: a monster hits you for 3 Damage and you take an Injury. With
  the main rules you still lose 1 Life, or maybe you can't use an Injury at all
  on a 3-point loss. With the combat rules you lose 0.

  Evidence in the book: the Zombie Tiger says "if you already have 3 Injuries,
  lose 3 Life instead". That suggests one Injury can be worth 3 Life, which
  supports the combat reading.

  Where to look:
  - Book: the Life and Sanity sections and the "Mental Scars and Injuries"
    section in the combat chapter, next to the Insanity and Injury tables (p. 35
    per the table of contents). Also check the pregenerated characters page and
    any errata.
  - Web: "Alone Against Fear" injury table how many life points, "Alone Against 
    Fear" mental scar prevent sanity loss rules question, plus
    site:boardgamegeek.com "Alone Against Fear", where the rules forums are. I
    believe the game is by Andrea Sfiligoi (Ganesha Games), so his name or the
    publisher's might help. The Four Against Darkness Facebook group and Reddit
    are other likely places for author answers.

  3. Expert cost

  - Book: the Experience section (gameRules/1_1_main_rules.md:144) only says
    "You may also spend XP to bring a Skill you already have to Expert level",
    with no price. The Skills chapter intro gives no price either. The Arcane
    Skills intro says some "may be learned twice", which hints that the Expert
    level is bought like a second purchase. The pregenerated characters page
    might show an example.
  - Web: "Alone Against Fear" expert skill XP cost, or "Four Against Darkness" 
    "Alone Against Fear" upgrade skill expert.

  4. "Derived values are computed, not stored"

  On the paper sheet, when you learn Tough mid-game, you erase "max Life 10" and
  write "12". When you take the Shaky scar, you erase your max Sanity and write
  one less. Max Life is really a calculation: base (8 + creation points) +
  Tough + any other effect. The same goes for max Sanity and the carry limit (10
  + backpack + Strong).

  In domain terms, the sheet only needs the base values plus the list of 
  effects. Max Life, max Sanity and the carry limit are always recalculated from
  them, so they can't get out of sync when an effect is gained or lost
  (learning Tough, losing the backpack). I'll reword that sentence in the doc
  this way.

  The Contradiction in the Book:                                                
                                                                                
  1. The section you quoted: In the summary/quick-reference section, the text   
  says you can negate "all Damage from a single attack by rolling on the Injury 
  table."                                                                       
  2. The Character Creation / Core Rules section: Earlier in the book (under    
  the character stats explanation), the text explicitly states: "During the     
  game, you may prevent the loss of 1 or 2 Life points by rolling on the Injury 
  table."                                                                       
                                                                                
  How the Community and Creator resolve it:                                     
                                                                                
  Because of this poor phrasing, the consensus among players is to follow the 1 
  or 2 points maximum rule.                                                     
  If it could absorb all damage, a single physical injury could save you from a 
  massive 6-damage boss attack. In a brutal survival-horror game like this,     
  allowing a player to entirely wipe away a massive attack with just one of     
  their three lifetime injury slots makes the game significantly easier than    
  intended.                                                                     
                                                                                
  The Recommendation for Your Game:                                             
                                                                                
  To keep the gritty, terrifying atmosphere of Alone Against Fear, you should   
  play it the strict way: The roll can only absorb up to 2 points of that       
  damage. If an attack hits you for 4 damage, you roll for an injury (absorbing 
  2), and your character sheet still takes the remaining 2 damage directly to   
  your Life points.

  The True Rule: Specific Overrides General                                     
                                                                                
  In tabletop game design, when two rules collide, the standard procedure is    
  that the specific rule overrides the general rule.                            
  1. The General Rule (The page you found): This is located in the              
  quick-summary overview at the back of the book. It uses broad, generalized    
  language stating you can negate "all damage from a single attack."            
  2. The Specific Rule (Character Creation/Stats section): This section defines 
  the exact, mechanical parameters of how the Stat functions. It explicitly     
  states: "you may prevent the loss of 1 or 2 Life points by rolling on the     
  Injury table."                                                                
  Because the 1-or-2-point limit assigns explicit numerical constraints to the  
  mechanic, it functions as the specific operational limit. The phrase          
  "negating all damage" in the summary is a generalized description of the      
  action (i.e., you use it to avoid taking the damage to your hit points), but  
  it is governed by the specific maximum of 2 points.

  Andrea Sfiligoi (the author) designs the Four Against Darkness / Alone        
  Against... series using a minimalist, old-school layout. These books are      
  notorious for editing oversights where quick summaries use looser language    
  than the specific rules chapters. When tracking similar design choices across 
  his rulebooks, mechanical numerical limits (like "1 or 2 points") are always  
  the intended math boundaries to keep the game balance intact.

    1 Life or 1 Sanity. So a Safe House is a box status plus the healing its
    source gave it.

    infection, disease and wounds, several missions, the Absent Mindedness scar,
